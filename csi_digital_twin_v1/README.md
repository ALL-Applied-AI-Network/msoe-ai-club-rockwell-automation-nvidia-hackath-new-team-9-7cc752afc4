# Automated Defect Detection via Synthetic Digital Twins

## Executive Summary

**Project Goals & Approach**
Our primary goal was to engineer a fully automated visual inspection pipeline capable of identifying specific product defects on a high-speed manufacturing line. Faced with asynchronous teammate coordination and a strict 48-hour hackathon deadline, our approach pivoted toward maximizing script automation. Rather than relying on manual data collection or physics-based simulation tweaking, we engineered a headless Isaac Sim pipeline that algorithmically injects defects (missing caps, crooked assemblies, crushed vials) into digital twin trays. This allowed a single engineer to rapidly generate annotated datasets and validate a Roboflow computer vision model within the compressed timeframe.

**What problem are you solving in manufacturing?**
Traditional defect detection relies either on manual human inspection, which is prone to fatigue, or on rigid programmatic vision systems that fail when lighting or minor variables change. We are solving the bottleneck of quality assurance scalability by utilizing synthetic data to train adaptable vision models, ensuring that anomalies are caught instantaneously before moving down the line.

**How does your project use physical AI?**
This project bridges the virtual-to-real gap inherent in physical AI. By utilizing a physically accurate USD environment, we synthesized the exact optical properties, camera angles, and geometric constraints of the real-world manufacturing station. The AI model trained on this synthetic data acts as a physical AI agent, capable of processing real-world camera feeds and making deterministic quality control decisions based on its spatial understanding of the digital twin.

**How did you use the physical equipment in the CSI?**
We utilized the Connected Systems Institute (CSI) digital assets, specifically the `tray_inspection.usdc` digital twin and the exact `CapSealCam` optics profiles. By manipulating the native XformOp translation and rotation matrices of these specific CSI equipment models, we accurately simulated how physical defects present themselves under the station's dedicated flash lighting.

**Evidence of Success**
Our codebase successfully automates the end-to-end inspection loop. The execution of `tools/run_full_pipeline.py` dynamically generates a batch of simulated trays with randomized defects, queries the trained Roboflow model via a serverless inference workflow, and parses the payload to draw accurate bounding boxes.
